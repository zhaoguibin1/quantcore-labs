How the numbers are checked

  • Against reference libraries. Calculators and snippets are cross-checked against SciPy, statsmodels, NumPy and pandas, or against closed-form results. For example, the implied-volatility solver is round-tripped over 466 grid cases, and the efficient-frontier optimum matches SciPy SLSQP to 1e-10.
  • On every rebuild. Each published Python file is executed in a fresh interpreter together with its assertions, so a dependency change that breaks a result stops the build.
  • Stated conventions. Units and day counts (Theta per day, Vega per vol point, 252 or 242 trading days) are written on each page, because most disagreements between tools come from conventions, not maths.
  • Public data only. The only external data is the U.S. Treasury par yield curve, which is public and refreshed each business day. Licensed market data is not redistributed.
