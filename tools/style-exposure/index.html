The page ranks every asset inside the universe and reports where your holdings sit, as a percentile from 0 to 100, both raw and against assets of similar size. Your data never leaves the browser.

Formulas and conventions

p_i,t = (rank x_i,t − 0.5) / n_t · 100 average ranks for ties; n_t = assets with a value on date t Exposure_t = Σ_i w_i,t · p_i,t / Σ_i w_i,t over holdings (w > 0) that have a value; 50 = same as the universe bucket_i,t = min(B − 1, floor(B · (rank size_i,t − 0.5) / N_t)) B equal-count size buckets p_adj_i,t = percentile of x_i,t among assets in the same size bucket (needs ≥ 20 values in the bucket) Exposure_adj_t = Σ_i w_i,t · p_adj_i,t / Σ_i w_i,t Coverage_t = Σ w of holdings with a value / Σ w of all holdings Summary = plain average of Exposure_t across dates; "Dates > 50" = share of dates with exposure above 50 Tilt label : ≥ 60 clearly high · ≥ 54 high · ≤ 46 low · ≤ 40 clearly low · otherwise near universe

The first column is treated as a date only when its header is date, dt, trade_date or day; without it the paste is one snapshot. A column named size, mcap, market_cap, float_mcap or log_mcap is pre-selected as the size column. A date is used only if the universe has at least the minimum number of assets and at least three holdings. Size adjustment needs 20 assets per bucket, so 200 assets for ten buckets. Negative or unreadable weights count as zero. If an asset repeats on a date the last row wins.

How to read the output

  • Read the size-adjusted column first. If a tilt is strong in the raw column and close to 50 after adjustment, the portfolio is holding small (or large) companies, not selecting on that characteristic.
  • Exposure is not return. A percentile of 75 on book-to-price says the holdings are cheap. It does not say cheapness paid. To split returns into style and selection you need a returns-based attribution on top.
  • Unintended tilts are the useful finding. A signal built on turnover and reversal can end up with a strong value tilt, or with low margins and high leverage as a side effect of cheapness. Those are risks nobody chose.
  • Check coverage. An exposure computed from 60% of the holdings weight describes 60% of the portfolio.
  • Stability matters. A tilt above 50 on every date is a design feature; one that flips sign is incidental.

Summary for AI agents (Markdown mirror)

# Portfolio Style Exposure Analyzer

> Client-side, holdings-based style analysis: each characteristic becomes a 0–100 percentile inside the pasted universe, and the portfolio's exposure is the weighted mean percentile of its holdings, raw and size-adjusted. URL: https://quantcore-labs.com/tools/style-exposure/

## Inputs

  • - Header row, then [date,] asset, weight, one or more characteristic columns; weight 0 = universe only
  • - Size column (optional), size buckets (5 or 10), weighting (by weight or equal), minimum universe per date

## Conventions

  • - Percentile = (average rank − 0.5) / n · 100 among assets with a value; 50 = same as the universe
  • - Size-adjusted percentile is computed inside equal-count size buckets with at least 20 values
  • - Summary = average across dates; tilt labels at 40, 46, 54, 60

## Reference check

  • - Raw, size-adjusted and equal-weighted exposures and coverage match a pandas implementation to 1e-12 on a table with ties, missing values and unequal weights

FAQ

What does a percentile of 50 mean?
The holdings look like the pasted universe on that characteristic. Every asset in the universe gets a percentile from 0 to 100, so the universe average is 50 by construction. A weighted average of 70 for your holdings means they sit, on average, at the 70th percentile.
Why two numbers, raw and size-adjusted?
Many characteristics are correlated with company size: small caps are usually more volatile, less profitable and cheaper on book value. A portfolio of small caps then looks tilted on all of them without selecting for any. The size-adjusted figure ranks each asset only against assets in the same size bucket, which removes that mechanical part.
What should the universe be?
The set of assets the portfolio could have held on that date, with the same filters the strategy applies: listing age, liquidity, risk-warning status. Percentiles are relative to whatever you paste, so a universe that is too narrow or too broad changes every number.
Which direction is 'high'?
A higher value of the column is a higher percentile. If low volatility is the style you care about, paste minus volatility, or read a percentile of 30 on volatility as a low-volatility tilt.
Is my data uploaded anywhere?
No. Parsing and every calculation run in your browser. The page makes no network request with your data.
