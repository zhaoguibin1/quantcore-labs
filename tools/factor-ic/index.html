Formulas and conventions

IC_t = corr( f_i,t , r_i,t→t+h ) across assets i on date t (Pearson) Rank IC_t = corr( rank f_i,t , rank r_i,t→t+h ) average ranks for ties (Spearman) Mean IC = (1/n) Σ_t IC_t n = dates used ICIR = mean(IC) / stdev(IC, sample) t-stat = ICIR · √(n / h) h = holding horizon in sampling periods Hit rate = share of dates with IC_t > 0 bucket_i,t = min(Q − 1, floor(Q · (rank f_i,t − 0.5) / N_t)) N_t = assets on date t G_q = mean over dates of the equal-weighted return of bucket q Spread_t = G_Q,t − G_1,t Annualised spread = mean(Spread) · P / h Monotonicity = Spearman correlation between q and G_q

The forward return on a row must be the return earned after the factor value was known: for a signal at the close of t that is traded at t+1 and held h periods, r = close(t+1+h) / close(t+1) − 1. A date is used only if it has at least the minimum number of assets with both a factor value and a return, and at least as many assets as quantile groups; other dates are skipped and counted. If the same asset appears twice on one date the last row wins. When every date parses, dates are sorted; otherwise the order of first appearance is kept and the per-year table is hidden.

How to read the output

  • Level and stability together. A mean rank IC of 0.03 with ICIR 0.5 is usually worth more than 0.06 with ICIR 0.15. The per-year table shows whether the sign holds outside one regime.
  • The t-stat is a first filter, not a verdict. It assumes roughly independent dates after the n / h correction. If the factor was chosen after looking at many candidates on the same history, a t of 2 is weak evidence.
  • Quantile bars should line up. An IC that comes only from one extreme bucket, or a non-monotonic pattern, points to a non-linear or outlier-driven factor.
  • The spread is before costs. It ignores turnover, trading costs, price limits and capacity, and the annualised figure is a simple scaling. Short legs are often not tradable at all.
  • Raw IC can be a size or industry bet. Neutralise the factor against industry and log market cap before pasting if you want the stock-selection part only.

# Factor IC Analyzer

> Client-side analyzer for rank IC, Pearson IC, ICIR, t-stat, hit rate, quantile-group returns and top-minus-bottom spread from a pasted factor panel. URL: https://quantcore-labs.com/tools/factor-ic/

## Inputs

  • - Rows of date, asset, factor value, forward return (decimal or percent); header optional
  • - Minimum assets per date (default 20), quantile groups (3, 5, 10), horizon h, periods per year

## Conventions

  • - Rank IC = Pearson correlation of average ranks (Spearman); ICIR uses the sample standard deviation
  • - t-stat = ICIR · √(n / h); hit rate = share of dates with IC > 0
  • - bucket = min(Q − 1, floor(Q (rank − 0.5) / N)); equal-weighted within bucket, averaged across dates
  • - Annualised spread = mean spread · P / h, before costs

## Reference check

  • - Rank IC, Pearson IC and bucket means match pandas rank(method="average") and SciPy spearmanr / pearsonr to 1e-12 on a panel with tied values

FAQ

What is a good IC?
There is no universal threshold. For daily or weekly cross-sectional equity factors a mean rank IC of a few hundredths that keeps its sign across years is already useful, because the fundamental law scales it by the square root of breadth. Stability matters more than the level: look at ICIR, the hit rate and the per-year table before the mean.
Why rank IC and not Pearson IC?
Rank IC is the Spearman correlation, so a handful of extreme returns or factor values cannot dominate it. Pearson IC is shown next to it; a large gap between the two usually means outliers are driving the linear correlation.
Why is the t-stat divided by the square root of the horizon?
If you sample every period but hold for h periods, consecutive forward returns overlap and the IC series is autocorrelated. Taking the effective sample as n / h is a simple correction. If your dates are already h periods apart, leave the horizon at 1.
How are the quantile groups formed?
On each date assets are ranked by factor value with average ranks for ties, and rank r out of n goes to bucket floor(Q (r - 0.5) / n). Buckets are equal-count up to ties, tied values always share a bucket, and returns are equal-weighted inside a bucket and then averaged across dates.
Is my data uploaded anywhere?
No. Parsing and every calculation run in your browser. The page makes no network request with your data.
