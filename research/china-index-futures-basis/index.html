China Index Futures Basis RESEARCH NOTE

CSI 500 and CSI 1000 futures have traded below their index for most of the past decade. This note measures what that discount was worth: what a long futures position in each of the four equity index futures on the China Financial Futures Exchange, rolled every quarter, earned against the total-return index from 2015 to 2026.

Key findings

  1. IC paid a large discount for ten years. From 2015-04-17 to 2026-11-16 a long CSI 500 futures position, rolled each quarter, returned +10.3% a year while the CSI 500 total-return index returned -0.7%: a gap of 11.0 percentage points a year, before any interest on the cash not needed for margin.
  2. The gap shrank, then came back. For IC it averaged +14.1% a year in 2016–2020 and +6.1% in 2021–2025, with only +0.8 to +4.0 points a year in 2022–2024 and +11.8% in 2025. IM, listed in July 2022, shows +8.8% a year.
  3. Large-cap futures carry little. IF (+3.3% a year) and IH (+0.3%) earned far less. Since 2021 IF has moved between −2.3 and +3.2 points a year, and IH was negative in four of those six years.
  4. It is an equity position. The rolled long moves with the index. IC fell 52% from June to September 2015, and fell more than a third again from 2017 to 2018 and from 2022 to early 2024. The discount does not protect against that.
  5. Hedgers pay the same gap. Holding stocks and shorting IC to remove market risk cost roughly the IC gap above, which is why hedged stock-selection strategies in China need a large stock-picking edge before they earn anything.

Why the futures trade at a discount

A fairly priced index future sits close to the index plus financing cost minus expected dividends over its life. In China the small and mid-cap futures have traded well below that level for most of the period. The explanations usually given are:

  • One-sided hedging demand. Market-neutral funds hold baskets of small and mid-cap stocks and sell IC and IM futures to remove market risk. Their selling is persistent and grows with the assets these funds manage.
  • Arbitrage is hard. Buying the cheap future and shorting the stocks would close the gap, but borrowing small-cap stocks is limited and expensive. Without that arbitrage the discount can persist.
  • Dividends. Contracts that span the May to August dividend season trade lower by the expected dividends. This matters most for IH and IF, whose constituents pay larger dividends, and is a normal part of fair value rather than a discount.
  • Trading restrictions. After the 2015 crash the exchange raised margins and fees and limited intraday trading. Liquidity fell, and the annualised discount of the far IC contracts averaged more than 20% in both 2015 and 2016. The rules were eased in steps from 2017.

The study: rolled long futures against the total-return index

Method

  • Position. Long one futures contract with notional equal to capital (no leverage), held in a quarterly contract (March, June, September or December). Three trading days before expiry it is rolled into the next quarterly contract with more than 45 calendar days left.
  • Prices and costs. Daily closing prices, from Sina Finance and, before 2019, CFFEX daily reports. Each roll pays 2 basis points per side on notional. No interest is credited on the cash not used as margin.
  • Benchmark. The CSI total-return index of the same underlying (dividends reinvested): H00905 for IC, H00852 for IM, H00300 for IF and H00016 for IH.
  • Gap. Annual return of the rolled long minus the annual return of the total-return index. Because no interest is credited, a fairly priced future would show a gap of roughly minus the short-term interest rate, about −1.5% to −3% a year over this period. A positive gap is therefore the discount captured above fair value, net of that cost.

Risks and practical points

  • Leverage is the main danger. Exchange margin is a small fraction of notional, so it is easy to hold more notional than capital. A position at three times capital would have lost all its equity in IC during 2015, again from late 2017 to 2018, and from 2022 to February 2024, before counting margin calls.
  • Keep cash for margin calls. Even unlevered, the futures account needs a buffer well above the minimum margin, because a large down day can trigger a call before the remaining cash can be moved.
  • The discount can narrow. When hedging demand falls or arbitrage capacity improves, the basis moves toward fair value and a long position underperforms the index for that period, as in 2022–2024.
  • Rules change. Margin, fees, position limits and intraday rules have been changed several times since 2015, and changes can move the basis quickly.
  • Access. Trading these contracts in mainland China requires a futures account and passing the exchange's suitability requirements, which include minimum funds, a knowledge test and trading experience.

Data notes and limitations

  • Closing prices, not settlement prices; the settlement price is a volume-weighted average of the last hour and can differ from the close.
  • For contracts still trading at the end of the sample the expiry is taken as the third Friday of the contract month; postponements for exchange holidays are ignored.
  • The basis-by-maturity table uses the price index, not the total-return index, so contracts spanning the dividend season include expected dividends.
  • The study ignores taxes, margin interest, position limits and the 2015–2017 trading restrictions, and assumes trading at the close.
  • Past basis and past returns do not indicate future results.

FAQ

What is the basis of an index future?
The difference between the futures price and the index level, here expressed as a percentage of the index: F / S − 1. A negative basis means the future trades at a discount. Dividing by the days left and multiplying by 365 gives an annualised figure that can be compared across contracts and over time.
Why do CSI 500 and CSI 1000 futures trade at a larger discount than CSI 300 futures?
Market-neutral funds in China hold baskets of small and mid-cap stocks and hedge them by selling IC and IM futures, so selling pressure concentrates there. Arbitrage that would close the gap needs short sales of the stocks, which are expensive and limited for small caps. CSI 300 and SSE 50 futures have more two-way demand and cheaper stock borrowing.
Is the discount a free return?
No. A long future earns the discount only if the index does not fall by more, and it carries full equity risk. Part of the gap is also the normal cost of carry: a fairly priced future trails the total-return index by roughly the financing rate, because the buyer keeps the cash. The gap can shrink for years, as it did for IC in 2022–2024.
What do hedgers pay?
Roughly the same gap in reverse. Holding stocks and shorting IC to remove market risk cost about the IC gap shown here, which is why hedged stock-selection strategies need a large alpha before they earn anything.
Is this investment advice?
No. It is a description of historical data and a stated method. Futures are leveraged instruments and are not suitable for everyone.
